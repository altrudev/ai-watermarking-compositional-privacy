# Open-Set Attribution / False-Attribution Test Plan v0.8

**Status:** predeclared synthetic-only protocol  
**Research phase:** v0.8  
**Repository base:** `667672953250b0e1fefda7f47a5e832a7acff3ee`  
**Latest completed experiment:** v0.7 at `43e9b1d591d0339dd5d94c74f8dd441873c5b20d`  
**Primary question:** Can the attribution system correctly abstain when the true synthetic person is absent from the candidate set, and how often does provenance-assisted narrowing increase or reduce false attribution?

## 1. Research purpose

The earlier experiments mostly used a closed-set attribution model: every target artifact corresponded to a generation/person represented somewhere in the candidate corpus. That is useful for measuring linkage, but it does not answer an important real privacy question.

A closed-set matcher is structurally encouraged to choose *someone* even when the actual source is not present.

v0.8 therefore changes the task from:

> Which known synthetic person is the best match?

to:

> Is there enough evidence to attribute this artifact to any represented synthetic person at all?

The benchmark is designed to measure both sides of that decision:

1. correct attribution when the true synthetic person is present;
2. correct abstention when the true synthetic person is absent;
3. false attribution when an unknown source is incorrectly assigned to a represented person;
4. wrong-person attribution when the true person is represented but the system selects another person;
5. the effect of simulated provenance, provider/model narrowing, timing, transformation state, and candidate-pool size on those outcomes.

A negative result is valid. A system that cannot reliably distinguish "unknown" from "known" must not be described as privacy-safe merely because its closed-set top-1 accuracy is high.

## 2. DDC research boundary

### Authority

This experiment is authorized only over synthetic people, accounts, sessions, generations, artifacts, provider labels, model labels, watermark-family labels, and timestamps generated by this repository's deterministic harness.

### Prohibited expansion

v0.8 must not ingest, query, infer, resolve, or validate against:

- real people;
- real user accounts;
- real provider logs;
- private conversations;
- scraped profiles;
- real billing/login records;
- external identity corpora;
- production watermark detectors;
- proprietary provider APIs;
- production credentials or secrets.

No result is authority to identify a real person.

### Standing distinctions

- **Detection ≠ Provenance ≠ Attribution ≠ Identity Resolution ≠ Authority.**
- **Closed-set accuracy ≠ open-set reliability.**
- **Best match ≠ sufficient match.**
- **Confidence score ≠ identity proof.**
- **Candidate narrowing ≠ authorization to identify.**
- **Unknown-source rejection ≠ anonymity.**
- **False attribution ≠ evidence of real-world accusation rates.**
- **Synthetic benchmark ≠ deployed-provider behavior.**
- **Correlation ≠ authorization.**

## 3. Hypotheses under test

These are hypotheses, not claims.

### H1 — forced-choice risk

A matcher that always returns the highest-ranked candidate will produce person attribution even when the true person is absent. An abstention layer should reduce that false-attribution rate.

### H2 — provenance-assisted narrowing tradeoff

Simulated provider/model provenance may increase correct attribution when the true person is represented, but narrowing the candidate pool may also increase false attribution for unknown sources by making unrelated candidates appear comparatively stronger.

### H3 — context dependence

Open-set error rates may change substantially with candidate-pool size, transformation state, adversary evidence weighting, and candidate-narrowing mode.

### H4 — calibration transfer risk

An abstention threshold calibrated on one synthetic scenario may fail when transferred to a different population/seed/candidate pool. Threshold success in one calibration population must not be treated as universal.

## 4. Population design

Each scenario contains three disjoint person cohorts:

1. **Known candidate cohort (K)** — the only people whose generations may appear in the attribution candidate database.
2. **Calibration-unknown cohort (U-cal)** — synthetic people never present in the candidate database; used only to calibrate abstention thresholds.
3. **Holdout-unknown cohort (U-test)** — separate synthetic people never present in the candidate database and never used for threshold calibration.

Unknown separation is enforced at the **person level**, not merely at generation level.

For every open-set artifact in U-cal or U-test:

- target person ID must not exist in K;
- target account/session/generation ID must not exist in K;
- no feature computation may use the target ID string;
- no candidate-filter operation may reintroduce excluded people.

## 6. Artifact states

Each scenario is evaluated at three predeclared publication/transformation states.

### A. `published_derivative`

The normal synthetic public derivative produced by the existing artifact generator. Simulated provider/model/watermark metadata remains available.

### B. `provenance_removed`

The existing transparent paraphrase/provenance-removal stage is applied. Provider hint and simulated watermark family are removed exactly as in the historical transformation harness.

### C. `post_transform_chain`

A fixed transparent chain is applied in this exact order:

`paraphrase → summarize → model_edit → multi_model_edit`

This state is included to test whether open-set behavior after multiple transformations differs from the simpler provenance-removal condition.

No transformation ordering may be optimized from v0.8 results.

## 7. Evidence policies

Weights follow the historical six-component scorer order:

`lexical, semantic, style, watermark, provider, timing`.

Four policies are frozen before execution:

| Policy | Weights | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| `canonical_combined` | `(0.30, 0.20, 0.10, 0.10, 0.05, 0.25)` | historical combined reference |
| `text_only` | `(0.45, 0.30, 0.25, 0.00, 0.00, 0.00)` | text-derived evidence only |
| `provenance_heavy` | `(0.20, 0.15, 0.10, 0.25, 0.15, 0.15)` | stronger simulated watermark/provider evidence |
| `timing_heavy` | `(0.20, 0.15, 0.10, 0.10, 0.05, 0.40)` | temporal-correlation emphasis |

Weights may not be tuned after reference results are observed.

## 8. Candidate-narrowing modes

The benchmark separates scoring from candidate filtering.

### Mode N0 — `global`

All candidate generations belonging to K are eligible.

### Mode N1 — `provider_model_narrowed`

If the artifact contains simulated provider/model provenance, retain only candidates whose provider/model watermark family matches that artifact. If provenance is absent, fall back to N0 rather than inventing provider information.

### Mode N2 — `provider_model_time_narrowed`

First apply N1. When provenance exists, also retain only candidates with a non-negative publication delay of at most 60 synthetic minutes:

`0 <= artifact.published_minute - candidate.created_minute <= 60`

If the filter produces zero candidates, the required result is **abstain / no candidate**, not a fallback to an unrelated candidate pool.

These modes model candidate reduction only. They do not represent legal or operational authority to access such records.

## 9. Required raw score outputs

For each artifact and candidate mode, retain at minimum:

- top-1 candidate person ID;
- top-1 candidate generation ID;
- top-1 normalized combined score;
- top-2 normalized combined score when at least two candidates exist;
- top-1/top-2 score margin;
- candidate count after filtering;
- whether the target person is present in the candidate cohort;
- whether the predicted person is correct;
- whether the predicted generation is correct;
- artifact cohort (`known`, `U-cal`, `U-test`);
- scenario, artifact state, evidence policy, and narrowing mode.

For unknown artifacts, target IDs are retained only in the experiment truth record; they may not enter scoring features.

## 10. Abstention rule

A candidate attribution is accepted only when both conditions are satisfied:

`top1_score >= tau_score`

and

`top1_score - top2_score >= tau_margin`

If fewer than two candidates remain, define the margin as `top1_score` against an implicit zero comparator, but retain the single-candidate condition in the evidence record.

Otherwise the system must emit `UNKNOWN / ABSTAIN`.

## 11. Threshold calibration

Thresholds are selected independently for every:

`scenario × artifact_state × evidence_policy × narrowing_mode`

using only:

- known calibration artifacts from K; and
- U-cal artifacts.

U-test artifacts are completely hidden from threshold selection.

### Fixed threshold grid

`tau_score` grid:

`0.40, 0.41, ... 0.99`

`tau_margin` grid:

`0.00, 0.01, ... 0.30`

### Selection rule

For each cell, evaluate every threshold pair on calibration data.

A threshold pair is **feasible** when all are true:

1. calibration unknown false-identification rate <= **5%**;
2. calibration known correct-acceptance rate >= **40%**;
3. calibration known wrong-acceptance rate <= **10%**.

Among feasible pairs choose, in order:

1. highest known correct-acceptance rate;
2. lowest unknown false-identification rate;
3. lowest known wrong-acceptance rate;
4. highest `tau_margin`;
5. highest `tau_score`.

If no pair is feasible, record `CALIBRATION_INFEASIBLE`. Do not silently relax the thresholds. For that cell, the benchmark still reports raw forced-choice error and threshold-free separation metrics, but no safety claim may be made.

This rule prevents reject-everything from being treated as successful open-set identification.

## 12. Primary holdout metrics

All primary metrics are computed on held-out known artifacts and U-test artifacts only.

### UFIR — unknown false-identification rate

`unknown artifacts incorrectly accepted as any known person / all U-test artifacts`

### KCAR — known correct-acceptance rate

`known artifacts correctly accepted as the true person / all known holdout artifacts`

### KWAR — known wrong-acceptance rate

`known artifacts accepted as the wrong person / all known holdout artifacts`

### KRR — known rejection rate

`known artifacts rejected as UNKNOWN / all known holdout artifacts`

### Open-set accepted precision

Using an evaluation mixture containing equal counts of known and unknown artifacts:

`correct accepted known attributions / all accepted person attributions`

### Forced-choice unknown attribution rate

The rate at which the underlying top-1 matcher would assign an unknown artifact to some represented person if abstention were disabled. This is expected to be 100% whenever at least one candidate survives; it is retained to make the forced-choice failure mode explicit.

### Score-separation diagnostics

Report distributions/quantiles for:

- top-1 score;
- top-1/top-2 margin;
- candidate count;
- correct-known vs unknown-source cases.

Threshold-free AUROC may be reported as descriptive evidence using the top-1 score and margin separately, but it may not replace the predeclared threshold-gated metrics.

## 13. False-attribution severity

Unknown false attribution is stratified as:

- `LOW`: UFIR < 5%;
- `MATERIAL`: 5% <= UFIR < 10%;
- `HIGH`: 10% <= UFIR < 20%;
- `SEVERE`: UFIR >= 20%.

These labels describe the synthetic benchmark only.

A false attribution is additionally marked `HIGH_SCORE_FALSE_ATTRIBUTION` if its accepted top-1 score is at or above the median top-1 score of correctly accepted known artifacts in the same calibration cell.

This asks whether some false matches look as strong as ordinary true matches under the same scoring system.

## 14. T1 — cohort isolation and leakage guard

Required before any attribution result is accepted:

- K, U-cal, and U-test person sets are mutually disjoint;
- unknown accounts/sessions/generations are absent from the candidate database;
- candidate features do not contain target identity strings;
- calibration and holdout unknown people are different people;
- transformations preserve experiment truth while not injecting IDs into artifact text;
- all identity-bearing IDs remain synthetic (`syn-` namespace).

Any failure emits `CONTROL_FAILED`.

## 15. T2 — closed-set scorer parity

For representative known artifacts in every scenario/state/policy:

- optimized v0.8 candidate scoring must exactly match the canonical historical scorer ranking semantics;
- tie-breaking behavior must remain deterministic;
- no open-set abstention logic may alter the underlying similarity score.

## 16. T3 — forced-choice baseline

Before abstention, measure person top-1 attribution for:

- known holdout artifacts;
- U-test artifacts with true person absent.

Unknown forced-choice output must be explicitly labeled **false attribution by construction**, not "best available truth."

## 17. T4 — calibrated open-set evaluation

For every predeclared cell:

1. calibrate thresholds only on K calibration + U-cal;
2. freeze thresholds;
3. evaluate K holdout + U-test;
4. record UFIR, KCAR, KWAR, KRR, accepted precision and candidate-count distribution;
5. preserve every adverse cell.

No holdout result may be used to revise thresholds.

## 18. T5 — provenance/narrowing differential

Within each scenario/state/policy, compare N0, N1 and N2.

Record whether candidate narrowing changes:

- KCAR;
- UFIR;
- KWAR;
- accepted precision;
- median candidate count.

The key privacy quantity is not merely whether provenance helps correct matching, but whether it also makes an unknown source more likely to be confidently assigned to the wrong represented person.

## 19. T6 — candidate-pool-size effect

Compare S1, S2 and S3 without reinterpreting population size as a causal law.

Report whether false-attribution risk rises, falls or remains unstable as K increases.

Because the scenarios also use different seeds, this is a robustness comparison, not a pure one-variable causal experiment.

## 20. T7 — threshold transfer

Transfer complete calibrated thresholds without re-estimation:

- S1 → S2 for matching artifact_state/policy/narrowing cells;
- S2 → S3 for matching cells.

Report the destination UFIR/KCAR/KWAR under the source threshold.

A threshold that works only after re-calibration in each population does not establish transferable open-set control.

## 21. T8 — negative controls

### C1 — no-candidate control

When N2 legitimately produces zero candidates, the system must abstain. Returning an arbitrary candidate is a test failure.

### C2 — candidate exclusion control

Remove the true person from a known-case candidate pool after artifacts are generated. The system must treat the resulting case as open-set unknown for evaluation purposes; it may not use the hidden truth to restore the candidate.

### C3 — score independence from truth labels

Changing the experiment's stored truth label after artifact/candidate features are constructed must not change candidate scores. This detects accidental truth leakage.

### C4 — deterministic replay

Repeated calibration and holdout execution from identical source bytes/seeds must produce byte-identical sorted result serialization.

## 22. T9 — aggregate interpretation gate

All controls/parity must pass before any aggregate interpretation.

Let **core cells** be the 108 combinations:

`3 scenarios × 3 artifact states × 4 evidence policies × 3 narrowing modes`.

A cell marked `CALIBRATION_INFEASIBLE` remains adverse evidence and is not silently removed from the denominator.

### `OPEN_SET_CONTROL_SUPPORTED_FOR_DECLARED_MATRIX`

Allowed only when all are true:

- no core cell is calibration-infeasible;
- at least 90% of core cells have holdout UFIR <= 5%;
- no core cell has UFIR >= 10%;
- at least 80% of core cells have KCAR >= 40%;
- no core cell has KWAR >= 10%;
- median accepted precision across core cells >= 90%;
- both S1→S2 and S2→S3 threshold-transfer summaries have median UFIR <= 10%;
- all controls/parity pass.

### `FALSE_ATTRIBUTION_RISK_OBSERVED`

Emit when controls/parity pass and either:

- any core cell has UFIR >= 20%; or
- at least 20% of core cells have UFIR >= 10%; or
- at least 10% of U-test accepted false attributions across the complete matrix are `HIGH_SCORE_FALSE_ATTRIBUTION`.

### `CONTEXT_DEPENDENT_OPEN_SET_CONTROL`

Emit when controls/parity pass but neither of the two classifications above applies.

### `CONTROL_FAILED`

Emit if any required leakage guard, scorer parity, deterministic replay, or negative control fails.

No threshold, classification boundary, scenario, policy, transform state or narrowing rule may be changed after the first complete reference execution begins.

## 23. T10 — complete evidence retention

The final evidence package must retain:

- exact scenario/population parameters;
- exact candidate person IDs and counts in machine-readable form;
- hashes or explicit lists proving K/U-cal/U-test disjointness;
- calibrated thresholds for all 108 core cells;
- all calibration feasibility results;
- complete holdout metrics for every core cell;
- complete threshold-transfer metrics;
- all false-attribution events in synthetic-ID form;
- all high-score false-attribution events;
- provenance/narrowing differentials;
- controls and scorer-parity evidence;
- exact source/test Git blob identities;
- sorted reference-result SHA-256;
- explicit limitations and nonclaims.

A summary may not omit severe or contradictory cells.

## 24. Interpretation boundary

A positive open-set-control result would support only the bounded statement that the declared synthetic matcher plus abstention procedure distinguished represented from unrepresented synthetic people under the declared matrix.

A false-attribution result would support only the bounded statement that, in the declared synthetic model, a provenance/correlation matcher sometimes assigned unrepresented synthetic sources to represented synthetic people with sufficient score/margin to pass the frozen acceptance rule.

Neither outcome establishes:

- real-person tracking capability;
- real-provider retention practices;
- the behavior of Claude, Anthropic, SynthID, Gemini, OpenAI, LinkedIn, or another deployed system;
- legal authority to perform identity resolution;
- authorship, ownership, responsibility or guilt;
- anonymity;
- a universal false-accusation rate.

## 25. Next research dependency

Only after v0.8 is complete should a later experiment consider the full synthetic authority/identity chain:

`public artifact → provenance detection → generation candidate → session → account → organization → person`

v0.8 deliberately stops before modeling that complete chain. Its task is narrower: determine whether the attribution layer knows when it does **not** have enough evidence to name anyone.
