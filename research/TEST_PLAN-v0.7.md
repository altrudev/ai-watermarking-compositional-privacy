# Cross-Family / Cross-Policy Replication Test Plan v0.7

**Status:** predeclared synthetic-only protocol  
**Research phase:** v0.7  
**Canonical predecessor:** `c29b40db9000d3e0a49c2c25fadab215d3084480`  
**Predecessor result:** v0.6 pairwise non-commutativity mechanism

## Research purpose

v0.6 found that pairwise transformation-order effects strongly predicted full-path person-attribution variation inside one fixed synthetic transformation family and attribution policy (`Pearson r = 0.9282921071911276`).

v0.7 is an **independent replication / falsification experiment**. It must not merely rerun the same four transformations under another seed. It asks whether the pairwise non-commutativity mechanism remains useful when all of the following change:

1. the transformation family;
2. the adversary weighting policy;
3. the synthetic population size;
4. the generation seed;
5. the publication-artifact seed;
6. pairwise effects are estimated on one artifact partition and full-path outcomes are evaluated on a separate holdout partition;
7. pairwise effects are transferred between paired scenarios with different seeds.

A mixed or negative result is valid and must be preserved.

## DDC research boundary

### Authority

The experiment is authorized only over identities and text generated by the repository's existing synthetic harness.

### Prohibited expansion

v0.7 must not ingest or query:

- real people or real accounts;
- real provider logs or private conversations;
- scraped social profiles or external identity corpora;
- production watermark detectors;
- proprietary translation/model APIs;
- production credentials or secrets.

No result may be used as authority to perform real identity resolution.

### Standing evidence rules

- Privacy transformation ≠ privacy evidence.
- Failed re-identification ≠ proven anonymity.
- Synthetic benchmark mechanism ≠ deployed-provider mechanism.
- Pairwise predictor success ≠ universal privacy law.
- Same final artifact class ≠ same privacy lineage.
- Replication failure ≠ permission to tune the protocol after observing results.
- Negative and context-dependent results must be retained.

## Fixed historical boundary

Historical v0.1–v0.6 experiment source/result files are immutable inputs to this phase. v0.7 must be additive.

The canonical v0.6 result is used only as the hypothesis source and threshold precedent. v0.7 must not change v0.6 thresholds or rewrite its result.

## Attribution scorer

The canonical v0.3/v0.6 six-component scorer remains the reference scoring semantics:

1. lexical similarity;
2. semantic similarity;
3. style similarity;
4. simulated watermark-family match;
5. provider match;
6. timing correlation.

A cached evaluator may be used only if exact parity with the canonical scorer is demonstrated in tests.

## Transformation families

v0.7 defines **two new transparent deterministic families**, each containing four operations. Neither family may simply alias the v0.6 `paraphrase / summarize / translate / model_edit` set.

### Family A — structural / normalization transforms

1. `lexical_generalize` — deterministically generalize selected content terms while preserving topic class.
2. `style_flatten` — remove declared synthetic signature phrases/hedges and normalize punctuation without changing identity metadata.
3. `sentence_rotate` — deterministically rotate sentence order while retaining all sentences.
4. `bounded_compress` — fixed-position extractive compression with a declared retention rule.

### Family B — representation / segmentation transforms

1. `topic_abstraction` — replace topic-specific terms with deterministic topic-category representations.
2. `clause_reorder` — deterministically reorder clause segments inside sentences where the grammar proxy permits it.
3. `segment_rechunk` — deterministically merge/split sentence segments without external generation.
4. `lexical_substitute` — deterministic transparent substitution from a fixed research lexicon distinct from the historical paraphrase map.

Every transformation must be deterministic, source-visible, synthetic-only, and independently testable.

## Scenario matrix

Six scenarios are predeclared. They intentionally vary population size and both deterministic seeds.

| Scenario | Persons | Generation seed | Artifact seed |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| S1 | 8 | 41 | 7000 |
| S2 | 8 | 73 | 9001 |
| S3 | 12 | 41 | 9001 |
| S4 | 12 | 101 | 7000 |
| S5 | 16 | 73 | 7000 |
| S6 | 16 | 101 | 9001 |

Scenario-transfer pairs are frozen as:

- S1 → S2;
- S3 → S4;
- S5 → S6.

## Artifact partition

Within every scenario, artifacts are deterministically partitioned before scoring using a stable SHA-256 hash of `target_generation_id`:

- even low bit → calibration partition;
- odd low bit → holdout partition.

The partition rule may not depend on attribution outcome.

Pairwise directional effects are estimated **only on calibration artifacts**. Full-path prediction is evaluated **only on holdout artifacts** for the primary within-scenario replication metric.

## T1 — transformation determinism and boundedness

For every new transform:

- repeated application to the same declared input is deterministic;
- target generation identity is preserved;
- no external data source is used;
- provider/watermark metadata changes only when explicitly declared by that transform;
- no real-identity loader exists;
- output remains non-empty for the reference population.

## T2 — pairwise non-commutativity map

For each family and scenario, evaluate all six unordered pairs in both orders on calibration artifacts.

Record:

- final-text difference fraction;
- final metadata equality;
- lexical / semantic / style feature divergence;
- signed person-top-1 order effect under every adversary policy;
- generation-top-1 order effect;
- largest changed evidence channel.

Zero effects must be retained.

## T3 — holdout full-path prediction

For each family × scenario × adversary policy cell:

1. estimate the six pairwise directional person-attribution effects from calibration artifacts only;
2. evaluate all 24 complete four-transform permutations on holdout artifacts only;
3. score each full path by the signed sum of its six pairwise ordering relations;
4. compute Pearson correlation between the pairwise score and observed holdout person-top-1 attribution.

This yields **60 holdout replication cells**:

`2 families × 6 scenarios × 5 policies`.

Per-cell interpretation:

- `r >= 0.70` → predictive;
- `0.30 <= r < 0.70` → partial;
- `r < 0.30` → not predictive.

## T4 — cross-scenario transfer

For each family × adversary policy and each frozen scenario pair:

1. estimate pairwise directional effects on the source scenario's calibration partition;
2. do not re-estimate effects in the destination scenario;
3. use the source pairwise effects to score all 24 destination full paths;
4. compare those scores with observed destination holdout person attribution.

This yields **30 transfer cells**:

`2 families × 5 policies × 3 scenario transfers`.

Transfer interpretation:

- `r >= 0.50` → transfer-supported;
- `0.20 <= r < 0.50` → weak/context-dependent transfer;
- `r < 0.20` → transfer not supported.

The lower transfer threshold is predeclared because pairwise effects are intentionally frozen across a changed synthetic population/seed.

## T5 — commuting negative control

For every scenario and adversary policy, verify the commuting control:

`lowercase → normalize_whitespace`

versus

`normalize_whitespace → lowercase`.

Required conditions:

- final text equality: true;
- final metadata equality: true;
- person-top-1 difference: 0;
- generation-top-1 difference: 0.

Any failure emits `CONTROL_FAILED` for the aggregate v0.7 claim.

## T6 — historical scorer parity

At least one path in every scenario must be evaluated with both:

- the v0.7 cached evaluator; and
- the canonical historical evaluator.

The complete metric object must match exactly.

## T7 — aggregate claim gate

Let:

- `H` = number of 60 holdout cells with `r >= 0.70`;
- `T` = number of 30 transfer cells with `r >= 0.50`;
- `median_holdout_r` = median of all 60 holdout correlations;
- `median_transfer_r` = median of all 30 transfer correlations.

The harness may emit only:

### `MECHANISM_REPLICATED_WITH_TRANSFER_FOR_DECLARED_MATRIX`

Required simultaneously:

- every negative control passes;
- scorer parity passes in every scenario;
- `H >= 42` (70% of holdout cells);
- `T >= 15` (50% of transfer cells);
- `median_holdout_r >= 0.70`;
- `median_transfer_r >= 0.50`;
- each transform family has at least one predictive holdout cell in every scenario;
- each adversary policy has at least one transfer-supported cell.

### `CONTEXT_DEPENDENT_REPLICATION`

Use when controls/parity pass and either:

- `30 <= H < 42`; or
- `9 <= T < 15`; or
- the median thresholds above are not jointly satisfied despite substantial positive cells.

### `MECHANISM_NOT_REPLICATED`

Use when controls/parity pass but:

- `H < 30`; or
- `T < 9`.

### `CONTROL_FAILED`

Use if any required control or scorer-parity check fails.

No threshold may be changed after the first reference matrix is generated.

## Interpretation boundary

A positive result would support only this bounded statement:

> Pairwise transformation non-commutativity remains predictive of full-path synthetic attribution variation across the declared new transformation families, adversary policies, and scenario matrix, including some transfer to changed synthetic populations.

A mixed result would establish context dependence rather than failure of the research program.

A negative result would falsify broad replication under the declared matrix and indicate that the strong v0.6 mechanism is family-, policy-, or population-specific.

None of these outcomes establishes anonymity, deployed-provider behavior, real-person attribution capability, or a universal privacy law.
